## The entry

> Day 2 stuck in the gym treadmill belt. 14 miles in, still going nowhere. This is not the cardio I was knitted for.

One line, 114 characters — a status update from a lost sock that has ended up somewhere it should not be: wedged in a gym treadmill belt.

## Why this line wins

- **It is unmistakably a status update from a lost sock.** "Day 2" opens like a dispatch from exile, and the whole line is written in the sock's own voice — present tense, first person, reporting in.
- **The premise is instantly clear.** One sentence places the sock (stuck in the treadmill belt), the next gives the absurd consequence (14 miles run, zero progress), and the closer lands the joke. No setup is wasted.
- **It sounds like a sock.** "Knitted for" is the tell — only a sock would frame its suffering as a manufacturing complaint. The sock was literally made for feet and motion, which makes its fate funnier.
- **It dodges the obvious sock material.** No dryer, no lint, no dust bunnies, no hand puppets, no vacuum cleaner, no partner who remarried a glove. The treadmill is fresh territory: a sock built for running, condemned to run forever and arrive nowhere — the one cardio fate worse than being lost.
- **It earns the laugh with escalation, not cliché.** The joke builds in three beats — location, mileage, verdict — and the final line reframes the whole thing as a worker's grievance against its own design.
